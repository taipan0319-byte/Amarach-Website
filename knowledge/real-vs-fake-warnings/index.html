How to tell a real Microsoft warning from a fake one

The fake ones have one goal: to make you call a phone number or click a button right now. Real Windows messages don’t work that way, because Microsoft has nothing to sell you in a panic.

Signs it’s fake

  • It has a phone number. This is the clearest one. Microsoft error messages never include a phone number and never ask you to call. Anything that says “Call Microsoft Support at…” is a scam, every time.
  • It’s loud. A voice, an alarm sound, a beeping tone. Windows doesn’t talk to you about viruses.
  • It fills the screen and won’t close. Fake warnings are usually just a web page in your browser gone full-screen, designed so the close button is hidden.
  • It’s urgent and frightening. “Your computer is locked.” “Your data will be deleted in 5 minutes.” “Do not shut down your computer.” Real problems are almost never this dramatic, and real messages don’t threaten.
  • The spelling is slightly off, or it names things like “Windows Defender Security Center Alert Department.”

What a real one looks like

Real Windows notifications are quiet: a small message near the clock, in the corner of the screen. Windows Update asks to restart. Windows Security might report that it handled a threat. Note the tense: handled, past. Calm, small, and no phone number.

What to do when a fake one appears

  1. Don’t call the number. Don’t click anything in the window, including its “Close” or “Cancel” buttons, which may not be what they say.
  2. Close the browser: press Ctrl+W, or Alt+F4. If it won’t close, press Ctrl+Shift+Esc, find your browser in Task Manager, and click End task.
  3. If the browser offers to restore pages when you reopen it, decline.
  4. Nothing happened to your computer by seeing the page. If you did click, call, or let someone connect remotely, shut the machine down and call us. That changes the situation and it’s fixable, but it should be looked at.
