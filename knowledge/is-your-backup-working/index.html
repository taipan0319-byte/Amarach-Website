Is your backup actually working? Three things to check

Most businesses that lose data had a backup. It was set up years ago, it said it was running, and when the day came, it turned out to have been quietly failing for months. The fix is not a better product. It’s checking three things, occasionally, on purpose.

1. When did it last actually run?

Open your backup software and find the date of the last successful backup, not the last attempt. If that date is a surprise, that’s the whole problem. Backups fail silently more often than people expect: a full destination drive, a changed password, a folder that moved, an update that stopped the service. Set a calendar reminder to look once a month; it takes two minutes.

2. Is it backing up the right things?

Backups guard the folders they were pointed at, which are the folders that mattered when it was set up. Since then, you may have added new software, moved to a new shared drive, or started keeping files somewhere new. The one that bites hardest: business software databases. Your practice management or accounting data often lives in its own location, sometimes on a server, sometimes needing its own backup routine entirely. Copying the program’s folder is not the same as backing up its database.

3. Can you actually get a file back?

Pick one file. Restore it. That’s the whole test, and almost nobody does it. A restore test proves the backup is readable, the software works, and, just as important, that someone knows how to do it under calm conditions rather than learning during an emergency.

The shape of a good setup

The standard worth aiming at is 3-2-1: three copies of your data, on two different kinds of storage, one of them off-site. An external drive next to the computer protects you from a dead hard drive; it does not protect you from theft, fire, or ransomware that encrypts everything the computer can reach. That’s what the off-site copy is for.

Backup verification is part of every ongoing support arrangement we run. If yours hasn’t been checked since it was installed, that’s a call worth making this week rather than someday.
