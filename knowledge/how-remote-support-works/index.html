How remote support sessions work

Most issues don’t need a site visit. A remote session lets us see your screen and operate your machine from here, with you watching, and most problems that would once have been “sometime Thursday” become “give me twenty minutes.”

How a session starts

  1. You call or email about a problem.
  2. We send you a link, or walk you through opening the remote support tool.
  3. You approve the connection. It does not happen without you.
  4. We work on the machine while you watch. You can see everything we do, ask questions while we work, and end the session at any time.

What remote support is good for

Software problems, settings, email trouble, printer queues (often; printing is its own adventure), slowness, mystery pop-ups, updates, and “it was working yesterday.” It’s also how routine maintenance happens for clients on standing arrangements: quietly, at agreed times, without interrupting anyone’s day.

What it can’t do

Hardware. If a drive is failing, a fan is grinding, or a machine won’t power on, that needs hands, and Rockford-area clients get those visits from us directly, not from a shipped box and a mailing label.

The security part, plainly

  • Sessions happen when you initiate them. We don’t have a standing open door into your machines unless we’ve set up managed access together, deliberately, as part of an ongoing arrangement, and you’ll know if we have.
  • You see the session while it happens. Nothing occurs off-screen.
  • We will never cold-call you and ask to connect to your computer. Nobody legitimate does that. If someone calls claiming to be “your IT” and asking for access, and you didn’t call first, hang up and call us at the number you already have.
