A computer that was fast three years ago and is slow today almost never got slower. It got busier. The hardware is doing exactly what it did on day one — it's just doing it for more programs than you know you have.

## Where the speed actually goes

Every program you've installed since the machine was new had a chance to add itself to the startup list. Printer utilities, update checkers, cloud sync tools, that PDF program you used once in 2023 — many of them launch when Windows does and sit in the background holding memory. None of them is large by itself. Twenty of them together is why the fan runs while you're doing nothing.

The other common causes, roughly in the order we find them:

- **Startup clutter** — the list above. This is the big one.
- **A conventional hard drive** — if your machine still has a spinning drive rather than an SSD, that alone explains slow boots and slow program launches. This is the one case where hardware really is the answer, and it's an inexpensive fix.
- **Antivirus overlap** — two security products installed at once, each scanning the other's activity.
- **A full system drive** — Windows needs free working space. Under about 15% free, everything drags.
- **Browser weight** — dozens of open tabs and old extensions. The browser is the heaviest program most people run all day.

## What doesn't fix it

Registry cleaners, "PC speedup" utilities, and most things advertised in a pop-up will not help, and several of them are the problem wearing a disguise. Buying a new machine also often doesn't fix it — a new computer arrives with its own bundled clutter, and if your files and habits move over, the slowdown follows within a year.

## What actually fixes it

A methodical cleanup: inventory what's installed, remove what isn't earning its place, prune the startup list to what you actually use, check the drive's health and free space, and verify updates and security are current. That pass is most of what a [Dave Perfect](/dave-perfect/) service is — it's the difference between a machine that's four years old and a machine that feels four years old.

If the machine has a spinning hard drive, moving Windows to an SSD is the single best dollar-for-dollar upgrade available, and it's usually worth doing on any machine you plan to keep.
